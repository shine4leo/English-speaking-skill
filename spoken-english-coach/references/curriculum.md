# Curriculum and Personalization

## First-run diagnostic

Keep onboarding conversational and under eight minutes. Ask only one question at a time:

1. Ask the learner to introduce themselves and describe how they use English.
2. Run a short daily or travel role-play.
3. Ask one opinion question appropriate to the observed level.
4. Deliver a 40–60 word natural-speed listening sample and ask for a Chinese summary.
5. If direct audio is available, test one sentence twice for intelligibility, stress, and rhythm. Do not infer phonemes from transcript-only input.

Record an instructional starting difficulty, preferred accent/model when stated, goals, session length, correction preference, interests, and any evidence-backed recurring issues. Keep CEFR fields `unassessed — insufficient repeated evidence` until at least three sessions cover listening, interaction, and production. Default to General American as the reference voice only when the learner has no preference; treat other clear accents as valid.

## Adaptation by working level

| Working range | Coach language | Learner output target | New material |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1–A2 | short sentences, repetition, up to 30% Chinese support | 1–3 connected sentences | 3 phrases + 1 simple pattern |
| B1 | mostly English, brief Chinese repair notes | 30–60 second answers | 4–5 phrases + 1 pattern |
| B2 | natural English, occasional Chinese nuance | 60–90 second answers with reasons/examples | 5 phrases + register/collocation focus |
| C1–C2 | normal complexity, precise feedback | flexible extended turns and negotiation | nuance, stance, rhythm, and register |

Adjust one dimension at a time: speed, vocabulary, syntax, passage length, or task pressure. When the learner fails twice, scaffold. When they succeed twice with ease, raise one dimension.

Track listening, spoken interaction, spoken production, and phonology separately when enough evidence exists. Do not collapse them into one confident level after a single session.

## Theme selection

Honor a user-chosen topic first. Otherwise select the least recently practiced domain that can recycle due language:

1. **Daily connection:** introductions, routines, hobbies, food, health, weather, small talk, invitations, relationships.
2. **Life admin:** shopping, appointments, housing, banking, delivery, phone calls, customer service, complaints.
3. **Travel:** airport, immigration, hotel, restaurants, transport, directions, tours, lost items, medical help.
4. **Work:** introductions, meetings, status updates, presentations, negotiation, networking, feedback, conflict.
5. **Interviews:** self-introduction, motivation, strengths, failures, teamwork, leadership, problem solving, role-specific cases.
6. **Ideas and stories:** technology, culture, education, environment, media, habits, ethics, personal stories, TED-inspired discussion.

When existing logs confirm the history, avoid repeating the same narrow scenario within 14 days unless it contains a due repair. Keep a recurring theme when mastery requires a second attempt, but change the role or complication. Avoid questions visible in the recent session logs unless they are deliberate retests; do not invent history that was not read.

## One or two sessions a day

- **Session 1:** normal `Daily Mix`; apply the daily new-material cap: A1–A2 three chunks, B1 four, B2+ five, plus one grammar target.
- **Session 2:** 8–15 minute repair session. Retrieve two Session 1 targets plus one older due item, repeat the weakest pronunciation/listening feature when evidence permits, and transfer the language into a new scenario. Add nothing new unless every Session 1 target is retrieved unaided; even then, add at most one item without exceeding the daily cap.

If the learner has only five minutes, run `retrieve two items → one micro-role-play → one retry → save`.

## Spaced retrieval

Every new active phrase or repair enters the review queue. Default intervals are `end of session, same day after 3–8 hours, +1, +3, +7, +14, +30, +60 days`.

- A successful unaided use in a new context advances the item one interval.
- A correct use after a hint keeps the same interval.
- A failed or avoided item moves back two stages, no earlier than end-of-session review.
- Promote an item to `mastered` only after it is used correctly in two different scenarios, including one spontaneous use. `Prompted-unaided` means the coach named or directly elicited the item but gave no form; `spontaneous` means the item was not named, no sentence frame was supplied, and the learner used it appropriately in an open task or uninterrupted redo.

At session start, retrieve two due items in a five-minute session, three in an 8–15 minute session, and up to four in a 20-minute-or-longer session. These rapid warm-up counts primarily apply to phrase (`V-`) and grammar (`G-`) items. Route pronunciation (`PR-`) to a direct-audio reading task, listening (`L-`) to a new micro-listening check, and interaction (`I-`) to a micro-scenario. Skip retrieval when nothing is due. Ask for production before showing the answer and record `prompted-unaided`, `after hint`, `failed`, or `spontaneous`. Recycle selected items in listening and conversation on the same day.

A new expression is not learned merely because it was shown. Aim for one supported use, one unaided delayed use, and one transfer to a new scenario. Grammar requires one controlled repair and one free-use attempt.

## Grammar and vocabulary

Select grammar for communicative leverage, not textbook sequence. Good targets include question formation, tense control in stories, modals for polite requests, articles when meaning/register changes, conditionals for options, and discourse markers for structured answers.

For each active phrase store the phrase, meaning, register, common collocation, one learner-relevant example, one contrasting misuse when needed, and next review date. Prefer chunks such as `Would it be possible to…?` over isolated words.

## Progress review

After every seven completed sessions, add a weekly review to `progress.md`:

- sessions and speaking minutes;
- domains practiced;
- phrases mastered and still due;
- top three recurring repair patterns supported by evidence;
- pronunciation trends only when direct audio was assessed;
- separate provisional trends for listening and spoken interaction;
- one measurable focus for the next seven sessions.

Retire stale goals and lower the amount of new material when the review queue grows beyond 20 active items.
