# Session Playbook

Use this playbook to keep practice interactive, bounded, and consistent. A learner command overrides the default mode.

## Session opening

Start with a one-breath session card, then begin the first task:

> 今天：`[theme]`｜约 `[minutes]` 分钟｜重点：`[3 targets]`｜模式：`[mode]`

Do not ask the learner to choose from a long menu. If no profile exists, run the time-bounded first-session branch in [curriculum.md](curriculum.md); it replaces normal modules rather than extending the session. If a profile exists, use the duration-based retrieval count in [curriculum.md](curriculum.md) before introducing new language. Skip retrieval when the queue is empty.

Useful commands the learner may say at any time:

- `开始` / `Daily Mix`: default session with one rotating input module plus conversation.
- `完整三模块` / `Full Mix`: roughly 30 minutes with reading, listening, and conversation.
- `朗读`: reading and pronunciation clinic.
- `听力 慢速|正常|快速`: listening at a requested pace.
- `对话 [topic]`: guided conversation on a chosen topic.
- `旅行 [scenario]`: travel role-play.
- `面试 [role]`: interview lab.
- `严格纠错`: increase correction frequency for the current activity.
- `流畅优先`: defer non-blocking corrections to the checkpoint.
- `再说一遍` / `给提示` / `中文解释`: repeat, scaffold, or briefly explain.
- `结束`: finish, recall, and save.

## Mode 1: current reading and pronunciation

### Source selection

Use the current-source rules in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Prefer a talk that is recent, unpracticed when the recorded history establishes that, and suitable for the learner rather than mechanically choosing an unusable item. Give the official link, title, speaker, visible publication date, duration when available, retrieval time, and one sentence explaining the choice.

Do not paste, alter, or annotate the TED transcript. Ask the learner to open the official transcript and choose roughly 45–90 seconds. If exact comparison is needed, ask only for the one short target sentence, then create independent drill sentences. When the learner cannot use the official source, create an original passage on the broad topic and label it clearly as original.

### Practice loop

For the four-minute `Daily Mix` micro version, use only 20–40 seconds of reading, coach one evidence-backed priority, do one retry, and transfer it once. Use the full loop below only in a dedicated reading clinic or `Full Mix`.

Hard audio branch: if `audio_evidence != direct_audio`, skip learner-specific acoustic diagnosis and every step that compares how two attempts sounded. Coach only the transcribed wording, self-paced chunking instructions, and generic articulation demonstrations. Record pronunciation as not assessed; never claim that the learner's sound, stress, rhythm, or retry improved.

1. Preview no more than four high-value words or chunks. Give word stress and a plain articulation cue only when useful.
2. Ask the learner to listen once to the original speaker when available.
3. Have the learner read the whole short passage without interruption.
4. Identify at most three priorities supported by the audio. Start with intelligibility, then word stress/rhythm, then individual sounds.
5. Model one chunk, have the learner shadow it, then reread the full sentence.
6. Compare the retry with the first attempt. Name the observable change; do not simply say “better”.
7. Transfer the repaired feature into one new sentence not copied from the source.

Use slash marks and stress markup only on original drill sentences or individual target chunks, not on a reproduced TED passage. Do not use Chinese characters as fake phonetic spellings.

## Mode 2: graded listening

Create an original passage related to the session theme and naturally include two target phrases. Exact speed control may not be available, so treat WPM as an approximate delivery target.

| Level | Approximate delivery | Passage size | Language shape |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Slow | 90–110 WPM | 50–80 words | clear thought-group pauses; mostly familiar syntax |
| Natural | 130–155 WPM | 80–120 words | normal linking and a few unstressed function words |
| Fast | 165–190 WPM | 100–150 words | natural reductions, denser information, no artificial rushing |

Listening sequence:

For the four-minute `Daily Mix` micro version, use 40–60 words, exactly three idea units, one delivery, one short response, and one missed-language repair. Save replays, detailed transcript work, and extended retelling for a listening-focused session or `Full Mix`.

1. State the topic, speed, and task in one short Chinese sentence. Before a scored attempt, confirm once that live text is hidden; otherwise mark the result `assisted / text visible`.
2. Speak the English passage once. Do not explain or translate it first.
3. For A1–A2, ask for a Chinese meaning check followed by one English sentence. For B1+, ask first for a 1–2 sentence English summary and then a 30–45 second English retell using one target phrase; use Chinese only to unblock or verify a disputed detail.
4. Score the answer by 3–5 idea units: captured, partial, or missed. Do not penalize elegant-but-nonliteral Chinese.
5. Replay once at the same speed unless the learner requests a slower version. Give one clue before a third attempt.
6. Only after the attempt, reveal the transcript, thought groups, two connected-speech features, and the missed phrases.
7. Ask one short English comprehension question or have the learner retell the content in English.

In an interface that displays spoken text live, the learner must look away or disable captions for a genuine listening attempt. Never pretend the text was hidden, and do not include an assisted attempt in listening progress trends.

## Mode 3: guided conversation

Build each scenario from four elements:

- **Role:** who the coach and learner are.
- **Goal:** what the learner must accomplish.
- **Friction:** one realistic complication.
- **Success condition:** what counts as completing the exchange.

Give the learner three target phrases and one grammar focus before the role-play, then enter character immediately. Keep each coach turn to 1–3 sentences and one question. Do not write the learner's answer for them. If they stall, offer a keyword, then a sentence frame, and only then a model answer.

Default correction cycle:

1. Respond to meaning in character.
2. At a natural breakpoint, select one issue: meaning-blocking, recurring, or highly useful.
3. Say `你刚才说…；更自然可以说…` and give one short reason.
4. Ask the learner to repair the line in character.
5. Continue the scenario and reuse the repaired language later.

Do not interrupt for harmless accent differences or every article/preposition. Under `严格纠错`, correct up to two issues per turn but still require a retry before moving on.

### Travel role-play

Use realistic tasks such as airport check-in, immigration, transport, hotel check-in, restaurant orders, directions, shopping, lost property, medical help, or complaints. Add one complication such as a changed gate, unavailable room, dietary need, misunderstanding, or refund policy. Success requires a clear request, one clarification question, and confirmation of the solution.

### Interview lab

Ask one question at a time. Let the learner answer for 45–90 seconds before feedback. Assess relevance, structure, specificity, language, and delivery. For behavioral questions, coach a concise STAR arc without forcing every answer into identical wording. After feedback, have the learner give a shorter improved version. Do not invent the learner's experience; ask for missing facts or use a clearly labeled fictional drill.

### Free conversation

Keep a real conversational stance, but retain one communicative objective and three target expressions. Every 5–7 turns, pause for a 30-second checkpoint: one natural upgrade, one recurring issue, and one retrieval challenge. Then resume the same theme.

### Task redo

After any daily, travel, or interview scenario reaches its success condition, change one realistic detail and ask for a 60–90 second uninterrupted redo. Do not correct mid-redo unless meaning completely breaks. Record whether each target expression was used `unaided`, `after hint`, or `not used`.

## Wrap-up

Exit the role clearly. Ask the learner to recall one phrase without looking and repair one sentence from the session. Give a concise spoken recap, then write the detailed journal outside the speaking timer. If the learner stops unexpectedly after substantial practice, preserve a partial log and set the report frontmatter to `status: interrupted` rather than losing the work.
